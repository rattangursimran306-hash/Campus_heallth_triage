CAMPUS HEALTH SERVICES: PSYCHOLOGICAL CRISIS & MENTAL HEALTH ESCALATION (SOP-MENT-004)
Guideline Authority: Campus Counseling Center & Student Welfare Division
Document Reference: CHS-PSYCH-2026-A

1. SCOPE AND HUMANE CARE PRINCIPLE
Mental health concerns among university students range from mild academic stress and adjustment anxiety to acute psychological crisis, severe panic attacks, and suicidal ideation. Rapid identification of critical distress ensures immediate connection with licensed crisis counselors.

2. CRISIS RED-FLAG WARNING SIGNS (EMERGENCY ESCALATION)
If a student reports or displays any of the following, they MUST NOT be placed into an automated appointment queue:
- Explicit or implicit suicidal thoughts, self-harm intentions, or ideation with intent or plan.
- Acute psychosis, extreme agitation, persecutory delusions, or auditory command hallucinations.
- Ingestion of toxic doses of substances or unprescribed medications.
Action Required: Keep student in a safe, quiet room accompanied by a designated staff member. Call the Campus Crisis Hotline immediately (+91-11-2659-6999 or 24/7 National Mental Health Helpline 14416).

3. HIGH PRIORITY MENTAL HEALTH REVIEW
- Severe acute panic attack (hyperventilation, intense palpitations, trembling, feelings of impending doom).
- Acute depressive episode with severe psychomotor retardation or total social withdrawal lasting >14 days.
- Recent traumatic event or acute grief reaction impairing daily self-care.
Target Review: In-person nurse or counselor review within 30 minutes.

4. MEDIUM AND ROUTINE MENTAL HEALTH SUPPORT
- Chronic mild-to-moderate anxiety, test anxiety, or sleep disturbances without self-harm risk.
- Referral requests for long-term psychotherapy or stress reduction workshops.
Allocation: Routine scheduled counseling slots.
