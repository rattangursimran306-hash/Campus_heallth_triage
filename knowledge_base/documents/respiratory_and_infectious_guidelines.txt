CAMPUS HEALTH SERVICES: RESPIRATORY & COMMUNICABLE INFECTION PROTOCOL (SOP-RESP-003)
Guideline Standards: WHO Influenza & Respiratory Virus Surveillance Guidelines & CDC College Health Protocol
Document Version: 3.1 | Reference: CHS-INFECT-2026

1. INTAKE & INFECTION CONTROL IN RESIDENTIAL CAMPUS SETTINGS
Due to close congregate living in university dormitories, infectious diseases (influenza, SARS-CoV-2, RSV, infectious mononucleosis, and meningococcal infection) present substantial outbreak risks. Early screening and triage prioritization prevent transmission chains.

2. IMMEDIATE DROPLET ISOLATION CRITERIA
Any student presenting with acute respiratory symptoms accompanied by:
- Temperature >= 38.0°C (100.4°F)
- Sore throat, cough, coryza, or loss of taste/smell
must be immediately provided with a surgical or N95 mask and directed to the Dedicated Respiratory Isolation Waiting Area (Annex B).

3. DIFFERENTIATING URGENCY IN RESPIRATORY ILLNESS
A. Emergency Escalation:
- Acute dyspnea, retractions, oxygen saturation <90% on room air, or persistent hemoptysis.
- Stridor, tachypnea (>28 breaths per minute in adults), or inability to lie flat.
B. Urgent / High Priority Review:
- Fever >39°C lasting over 48 hours without relief from antipyretics.
- Deep pleuritic chest pain aggravated by deep inspiration or coughing.
- Suspected bacterial pneumonia in immunocompromised or asthmatic students.
C. Medium Priority Review:
- Typical viral bronchitis or tracheitis: rhinorrhea, mild barking cough, low-grade pyrexia (<38.5°C), duration 2-5 days.
D. Routine Review:
- Residual post-viral dry cough persisting >14 days without fever, wheeze, or constitutional fatigue.

4. DIAGNOSTIC SUPPORT RESTRICTION
Non-clinical decision-support algorithms must NOT attempt to differentiate between viral strains (e.g. influenza vs Covid-19) based solely on symptom checklists. Prioritization serves strictly to manage clinic waiting queues safely.
