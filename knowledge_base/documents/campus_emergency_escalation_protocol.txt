CAMPUS HEALTH SERVICES: EMERGENCY ESCALATION PROTOCOL (SOP-EMS-001)
Authority: Campus Health Executive Committee & Emergency Medical Response Team
Document Version: 4.2 | Effective Date: Fall Semester 2026

1. PURPOSE & PRINCIPLE OF IMMEDIATE ESCALATION
The Campus Health Center is an outpatient ambulatory facility and is NOT equipped as an emergency trauma or acute resuscitation department. When a patient presents or self-reports symptoms indicative of immediate life-threatening physiological deterioration, clinical review and appointment queuing MUST be bypassed in favor of immediate transfer to the tertiary emergency hospital.

2. MANDATORY EMERGENCY ESCALATION TRIGGERS (RED FLAGS)
Staff and automated decision support systems must trigger immediate human escalation under any of the following clinical presentations:
- Severe Acute Chest Pain: Crushing, substernal, or radiating chest pain to the left arm, neck, or jaw, particularly when accompanied by diaphoresis (cold sweat), dyspnea, or nausea (potential Acute Coronary Syndrome).
- Severe Respiratory Distress: Cyanosis (blue discoloration of lips/fingers), inability to speak full sentences, audible stridor, severe wheezing unresponsive to rescue inhalers, or oxygen saturation < 90%.
- Severe Anaphylaxis: Acute onset of facial/lip/tongue angioedema, airway compromise, diffuse urticaria, and hypotension following allergen exposure.
- Altered Mental Status & Acute Neurological Deficits: Sudden loss of consciousness, focal weakness, acute facial droop, slurred speech (FAST criteria for stroke), or prolonged seizure activity.
- Uncontrolled Hemorrhage: Pulsatile arterial bleeding or profuse venous blood loss that cannot be arrested by direct pressure.
- Severe Trauma / Spinal Injury: High-impact falls, motor vehicular impact, or suspected cervical spine compromise.

3. IMMEDIATE ACTION STEPS
A. Call Campus Emergency Medical Services (EMS) at Extension 911 or direct hospital line +91-11-2659-6100.
B. Do not allow patient to walk or remain unmonitored. Escort to the Acute Stabilization Bay (Room 101).
C. Administer high-flow supplemental oxygen (10-15 L/min via non-rebreather) if trained personnel are present.
D. Keep automated external defibrillator (AED) on standby.
E. Notify the Chief Medical Officer immediately.
